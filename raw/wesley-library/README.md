# Wesley Library — source editions Wesley owned

Scanned primary editions from John Wesley's own library, held for future resources (e.g. a study of Wesley's use of the Fathers, or the *irrequietum* variant below). Not part of the cleaned Wesley text corpus.

## Catalogue

`maddox-wesleys-reading-london-2003.pdf` — Randy L. Maddox, "John Wesley's Reading: Evidence in the Book Collection at Wesley's House, London," *Methodist History* 41.3 (2003): 118–33. The survey of the surviving "John Wesley's Library" (City Road, London) and the source of the shelf-marks (C28, D29, etc.). Note: the collection is only a fragment — Wesley gave much of his library away — so *absence* from it proves nothing.

### Holdings relevant to *Wesley's Metaphysics* (with Wesley's own inscriptions)

- **Augustine, *Confessiones*, Cologne 1647 (Sommalius/Egmond) — C28.** Inscribed *"J.W. 1750 – a sinner born to die."* His personally-marked copy; reads *inquietum* (see below).
- **Charles Bonnet, *La Palingénésie philosophique; ou Idées sur l'état passé et sur l'état futur des êtres vivans*, 2nd ed., Munster 1770 — D29.** Inscribed *"J.W. 1772"* (vol. 1 missing). ★ Hard shelf-evidence for the Bonnet connection: he owned and marked the *Palingénésie* — on the past and future state of living beings — in 1772, as the general-deliverance / new-creation / resurrection sermons were being written (1781–85). Bears directly on **Ch6 (De creatione, scale of being, animal souls)** and **Ch10 (De novissimis, general deliverance, future state of the beasts)**. The book already leans on Bonnet via the *Survey*; this is independent proof of the live shelf.
- **Joseph Butler, *The Analogy of Religion, Natural and Revealed*, 3rd ed., 1740 — F11.** Relevant to Ch7 (providence / natural theology).
- **John Fletcher, *A Reply to… Toplady's scheme of Christian and Philosophical Necessity*, 1777 — K22.** Relevant to Ch7 (the anti-necessity front). NB: Fletcher answering Toplady — *not* Priestley's *Philosophical Necessity* of the same year.
- **Bartholin, *Specimen philosophiae naturalis* — student-era MS "collection" (summary) in Wesley's hand.** Early natural-philosophy engagement (lifelong-coherence claim, Ch1/Ch9).

**Not in the surviving collection:** More, Malebranche, Clarke, Norris, Boehme (the Ch5 interlocutors). Expected — the collection is partial; the book's case for those rests on the *Address to the Clergy* + the *Christian Library*, not shelf copies.

## Augustine, *Confessiones* — Cologne, 1647 (Sommalius / Egmond)

- `augustine-confessiones-cologne-1647-sommalius-egmond.pdf` — full scan (465 pp., Google Books, PD).
- `augustine-confessiones-cologne-1647-ocr.txt` — OCR text layer (17th-c. Latin; long-s ſ often reads as "f").

**Edition:** *D. Aurelii Augustini Hippon. episcopi Libri XIII Confessionum, ad 3 mss. exempl. emendati*, ed. Henricus Sommalius (Jesuit); Coloniae Agrippinae, sumptibus Cornelii ab Egmond et Sociorum, 1647. Shelf-mark **C28** in the catalogue of Wesley's library — the copy that stood on his home shelf.

### The *irrequietum* finding (2026-07-10)

*Wesley's Metaphysics* (Wroot Press, Ch. 5) quotes Augustine *Confessions* I.1.1 as **"irrequietum est cor nostrum"** — the reading in the Jackson edition of Wesley's sermon; John Owen and Garrigou-Lagrange also carry *irrequietum*. Outler notes the variant (Bicentennial vol. 4, p. 64) but could not identify the source.

### Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE

The source of Wesley's *irrequietum* has **not** been identified. Every edition checked — across four centuries and both confessional camps — reads *inquietum*:

- **The standard critical editions:** *inquietum* (Knöll, CSEL 33, 1896; Skutella, Teubner, 1934; Verheijen, CCSL 27, 1981).
- **Migne PL 32 / the Maurist text:** *inquietum* (`~/patrologia/sources/pl/tei/7270.xml`, col. 0661). A search of all 5,276 PL files finds **no** author quoting I.1.1 with *irrequietum* (the 2 PL hits — PL 155 crusade verse, PL 189 Peter the Venerable — are unrelated to the passage).
- **Wesley's own shelf copy** (Cologne 1647, Sommalius/Egmond, C28): *inquietum* (OCR p. 82; incipit confirmed).
- **Erasmus / Froben, Basel 1528–29** — the Protestant-humanist *Opera*, the text an Oxford man would use: *inquietum*. Verified from the MDZ scan (BSB `bsb11204297`, printed p. 46 = image 72, Confessions Book I cap. I): "…Quia fecisti nos ad te, & inquietum est cor nostrū donec requiescat in te." Evidence image: `froben-basel-1528-confessions-bookI-1-1-p46-inquietum.jpg`. (Full volume re-fetchable from MDZ via that stable ID; not downloaded whole — a 400+-pp. folio.)

So *irrequietum* is in **none** of the printed Latin editions — humanist (Froben), Catholic (Cologne 1647, Maurist/Migne), or critical. Wesley had *inquietum* in front of him on his own shelf and still wrote *irrequietum*. That Owen and Garrigou-Lagrange carry it too *suggests* a shared citation/memory tradition rather than an edition, but that remains a hypothesis. Verdict stands: the source is **unidentified** — almost certainly why it stumped Outler; it is not a textual variant of any standard edition.

**Only remaining lead:** the Louvain (1576) edition — unchecked, but derivative of the same humanist line that (per Froben) reads *inquietum*, so unlikely to differ.
